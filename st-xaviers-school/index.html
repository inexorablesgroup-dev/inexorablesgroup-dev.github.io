Who We Are

A full school, not a stopping point.

Many schools in the district end at Class VIII or X. Ours does not.

Nursery, primary, middle, secondary, senior secondary — the whole ladder, on one campus, under one standard.

Which means no anxious transfer year, no new uniform at fourteen, no starting again with strangers.

Teachers who taught your child in Class VI still know them in Class XII. That continuity is the point.

The CBSE Curriculum, Nursery–XII

The national curriculum taught the way it is meant to be taught: patiently, in order, concepts before marks. The same syllabus a child would follow in any metro, delivered here in Gonda.

Practical Science

Senior secondary science is not a reading subject. Children learn it by handling it — every prescribed practical performed, recorded and understood.

Sport & Open Ground

Room to run, on a campus set outside the town crush. A tired, happy child studies better — the timetable makes space for both.

Library & Reading Habit

English fluency is built by reading, not by drilling grammar. A quiet room, full shelves, and protected time in which nothing is asked of a child except to read.

Art, Music & Stage

Annual day, assemblies, competitions. A child who has faced an audience carries that steadiness into a board viva and every interview after it.

Mentorship & Values

Every class has a teacher who tracks more than marks — attendance, mood, friendships, the quiet drop in a child who was doing fine last term.

In the Classroom

Teaching that waits for the slowest hand.

The board syllabus is fixed; the pace at which a child meets it should not be. Lessons here are built to be revisited — explained, worked through, and explained again for whoever still has a hand up. A concept passed over in Class VII becomes a lost mark in Class XII, and we would rather lose the time now.

Science & Streams

Senior secondary, taken seriously.

Being a senior secondary school means the hard part happens here — Classes XI and XII, stream choice, board practicals, the year that decides where a child goes next. Families here do not have to send a sixteen-year-old to Lucknow for it. The stream conversation happens with teachers who have already known that child for a decade.

Beyond the Syllabus

Study habits outlast syllabi.

What a child carries out of Class XII is not the syllabus — it is the habit of sitting down and working without being asked. Reading time, revision routines, and quiet supervised study are treated here as part of the school day, not as something homes are left to arrange alone.

The Academic Ladder

Fourteen years, one school.

This is the full route a child takes here — from the first day in Nursery to the Class XII board result.

NURSERY & KG

The first years

Letters, numbers, and the far harder lessons — sitting still, sharing, asking. Children arrive at three or four and learn what a school day feels like before anything is expected of them academically.

CLASSES I – V

Primary — where the base is laid

Reading, arithmetic and English fluency. Almost every difficulty a student hits in Class X traces back to something skipped in these five years, so nothing here is hurried.

CLASSES VI – VIII

Middle school — subjects get teeth

Separate subject teachers, real science, real mathematics. The stage where study habits either form or do not — and where a mentor teacher watching closely matters most.

CLASSES IX – X

Secondary — the first board

Two years of consolidation ending in the CBSE Class X examination, with practice papers, revision cycles and steady pressure that arrives early rather than all at once in February.

CLASSES XI – XII

Senior secondary — choosing a direction

Stream selection, board practicals, and the Class XII examination that decides the next step. Guided by teachers who have known the student since childhood — not by a school they joined six months ago.

AND AFTER

Out into the world

A CBSE Class XII certificate that is read the same way in Lucknow, Delhi or Bengaluru as it is in Gonda — and a child prepared to use it.
