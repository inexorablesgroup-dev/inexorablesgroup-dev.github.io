A Message From the School

Excellence, with character.

A child is not a vessel to be filled at one fixed speed. Souvann’s Millennium Public School was founded on the opposite belief — that a child is curious and absorbent, and that the school’s task is to give that curiosity something worth absorbing.

So the teaching is deliberately differentiated. There is extra scaffolding for the child struggling with a concept, additional English for the child still finding the language, and harder problems for the child who finishes early. Three children in one classroom are not asked to be the same child.

Academics carry the day here, taught in smart classrooms and tested in working laboratories. But the school holds equally to the moral and ethical footing that makes the marks mean something — the aim it sets itself is not merely a good student, but a good citizen.

“Nurturing the inherent potential and talent of each child.”

Life at Souvann’s Millennium

Five things a school owes a child.

Not a list of features — a set of obligations the campus is organised around, from the first bell to the last bus.

Academics

The full CBSE course of study from Playgroup to Class X, taught in smart classrooms with concepts settled before marks are chased.

Pastoral Care

An infirmary on the premises, CCTV from the gate to the grounds, and transport that hands every child back to the route they arrived on.

Activities

Laboratory work, music, art and the ordinary business of standing up in front of a class — the hours that build a voice, not just a report card.

Sports

Large courtyards and play areas, used daily. A tired, happy child studies better — the timetable is built with both in mind.

Values

The moral and ethical grounding the school states as its own aim: a good citizen first, and a good student because of it.

Questions Parents Ask

Answers, straight.

Admission runs from Playgroup and Nursery up through Class X, subject to seats in each section. Mid-session transfers are considered case by case — carry the previous school’s report card and transfer certificate when you visit.

It begins with an enquiry or a walk-in at the office. From there the office explains what the class your child is applying for requires — the interaction or assessment, the documents to bring, and the fee structure. Ask for all three in the same visit.

The school runs its own transport across Gonda town and nearby localities. Share your address at the office and the nearest pickup point and timing will be confirmed for you.

Parent-teacher meetings, a diary that is actually checked, and a class teacher you can reach. If something needs your attention, you should hear it from the school first — not from your child.
